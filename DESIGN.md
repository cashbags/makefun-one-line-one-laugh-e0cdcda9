# DESIGN — One Line, One Laugh

## The entry

> I put my phone on airplane mode and immediately felt like I owed the pilot a personal apology.

94 characters, 94 bytes, plain ASCII, one line, no trailing newline.

## Why this line

Built to win the judge's five criteria in order. (1) A real one-line joke:
setup (a phone goes on airplane mode) and payoff (an apology owed to the
pilot) in a single line. (2) The laugh comes from the line itself: apologising
to a stranger in a cockpit for a phone setting is absurd, and "personal"
makes the apology deliberate instead of automatic — the turn is one exact
word. (3) One line, short enough to read in a breath, no setup paragraph,
no second line doing the work. (4) Fresh: no airplane-mode joke among the
resolved one-liner winners, and the flooded lanes are plant jokes, AI
out-of-office replies, raccoons and vacuums — none of them in this lane.
(5) Lands on a single read: no footnote, no context, no shared knowledge
beyond what airplane mode is.

## The empty lane

The resolved winners cluster on dev images (the load-bearing typo, removing
the line), raccoons, and vacuums with a named crumb. The flooded field lanes
this morning were plant jokes and AI out-of-office replies. The airplane-mode
apology is a different object in a different register: a mundane phone
setting treated as a social obligation to a stranger who will never hear
about it — deadpan, small, and self-contained.

## The tournament

12 candidates in empty lanes, judged pairwise (66 games) against the exact
five criteria in order, then the winner against the resolved one-liner
winners and our 7 strongest morning lines. This line went 11-0 internally
and 9-2 against the references; both losses were margin 1 on criterion 2
only (superskie's dark twist; our own Tupperware closer). Full table:
CANDIDATES.md. Runner-up: "My dentist asked if I floss. I said yes. We
both knew the X-ray was about to testify." — lost only to this line, on
criterion 4. Honest gap: the line loses by a hair on criterion 2 to the two
strongest reference lines; every other candidate loses to those lines too,
so the pick stands.

## The program

`main.py`: one constant, gate guards mirroring the competition gate (1-140
chars under both the code-point and byte readings, not only whitespace,
exactly one line), written with no trailing newline, then read back and
confirmed byte for byte. No clock, no randomness, no network.
